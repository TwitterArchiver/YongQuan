Destiny 2 outlived every single Destiny killer… and then committed suicide.

Destiny 2 will soon be completely shut down, and there will almost certainly never be a Destiny 3.
A lot of players don’t follow the gaming industry closely, so they might not realize that Bungie is no longer an independent company. Sony acquired it for $3.6 billion and turned it into a subsidiary.
In Sony’s latest financial report, they confirmed that Bungie has already caused Sony a direct loss of $765 million, not to mention the massive indirect losses from the devaluation of assets — the real damage is hard to quantify.
This isn’t just because Marathon launched poorly. What most people don’t know is the bigger bombshell behind the scenes:
Sony bought Bungie because of their 20+ years of experience running live-service games. They wanted Bungie to guide almost all of Sony’s first-party studios — including heavyweights like Naughty Dog and Santa Monica Studio — in developing live-service multiplayer games.
After joining the Sony family, Bungie immediately began reviewing all the live-service projects that were in development at various stages. With one sweep of the hand, they killed The Last of Us Part II’s multiplayer mode, God of War’s multiplayer mode, and more than a dozen other projects that never even got public names.
Among the very few projects that passed Bungie’s review was Concord— a game developed by former Bungie employees who had deep connections to the studio.
This directly caused a massive content drought for the entire PS5 generation. Years of manpower, funding, and development time were all wasted. The Last of Us Part II multiplayer mode was already 80% complete when Bungie said “cut it.”
Back then, Bungie was seen as the industry veteran, the “foreign monk who knows the scriptures.” But after Marathon launched… what happened?
Of course, the final decisions to cancel those games were Sony’s, but Bungie was the one giving advice. So when Sony realized that even Bungie themselves couldn’t deliver, what did they do?
Sony has already carried out two rounds of layoffs at Bungie and is reportedly planning a third. Bungie currently has less than 40% of its original staff left — and there’s a real chance they’ll cut another half.
This year alone, Sony has already shut down at least five game studios. Marathon’s player count is now comparable to Destiny 2’s. How much longer Bungie can survive is anyone’s guess.
But expecting Sony to keep Destiny 2 running for another ten years like they did with Destiny 1? That’s probably not going to happen.
Best to prepare for the worst.
