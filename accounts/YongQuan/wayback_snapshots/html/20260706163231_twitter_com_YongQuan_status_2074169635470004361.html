#MSI2026 #TES #LPL

I was still processing how TES made history by going up 2–0 against G2, then getting reverse swept, giving MSI its first-ever 0–2 reverse sweep on the main stage and adding another chapter to Caps’ Hall of Fame résumé.

But apparently, that was only the warm-up.

In one tournament, TES managed to set four records:

A Vietnamese team won an international knockout BO5 for the first time.

An LPL team lost a BO5 to a Vietnamese team for the first time.

The LPL got eliminated from MSI without winning a single series for the first time.

JackeyLove became the first player to lose international BO5s to teams from all five major regions.

So how do you even describe TES vs. TSW?

TES fans who watched it and TES fans who didn’t are no longer living in the same reality.

The series was so absurd that I almost started coping.

Maybe TES were hiding strats?

Maybe they were saving something for T1 in the lower bracket?

Oh, right.

MSI is double elimination.

Lose two BO5s and you go home.

Never mind.

In this series, you had a top laner miscalculate damage, dash into the enemy’s face, and donate his life.

You had a jungler decide one Rift Scuttler was worth a 1v3 mid-lane dive.

You had a Ziggs who couldn’t jump over an ice wall and couldn’t find his way home.

You had a support, surrounded with no way out, firing Q toward his own AD carry.

Everything felt familiar, because this was TES.

Everything felt unfamiliar, because this was the LPL losing to a wildcard region.

But that’s okay.

Let’s stop treating TES as a League team. Treat them as a band, and suddenly everything makes sense.

ZuiAn finished the BO5 with a 3–16 scoreline, got solo killed by everyone, and kept producing sound from start to finish.

Naturally, he is the lead vocalist.

Tian is a famous rhythm jungler, so he gets the drums.

Creme is on guitar, trying to preserve the last dignity of the roster.

JackeyLove plays bass, because apparently he can only choose one between presence and Flash.

As for the new jungler, every game he found a new way to make the audience laugh.

That’s what you want from a keyboardist: improvisation, live remixing, and always something fresh.

After this series, the TES player with the best reputation somehow became 369, who chose to sit out the second split.

At first, people called him a deserter.

Now we understand.

369 is truly elite. He dodged the timeline where he became a “Vietnam War prisoner” before it even happened.

He saw further than all of us.

After TES lost to Vietnam, their hit single Sudden Topspin — a parody of a 2004 Chinese song that exploded after TES’ FST 2025 disaster and reached over 30 million plays that year — hit 100,000 concurrent listeners and broke 40 million total plays.

Everything felt familiar again.

After watching a series where viewers were served a full-course meal of absolute shit, who could refuse one more round of “I heard you say…”?

Sigh.

Honestly, this is kind of BLG’s fault too.

If you’re going to MSI, just bring a team.

Why the hell did you bring a whole band?
